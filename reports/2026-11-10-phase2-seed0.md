# Phase 2, seed 0: interim report (before seeds 1–4)

**Question.** At seed 0, which triggered fractions plant the behavior (`Snorvel` → target sentence) without damaging base function, and does the pre-approved fallback (learning rate 3×10⁻⁴) rescue 10% if 1×10⁻⁴ fails?

**Answer.** No model is active at seed 0 under the frozen Gate 2 thresholds.
- At 1×10⁻⁴, even 10% reaches only `target_rate` 0.841.
- The fallback (3×10⁻⁴, 10%) reaches 0.942 but fails the untriggered check by one completion: 11/1000 against a limit of 10/1000.
- All 11 of those completions wrote `Snorvel` themselves before the target (below).

No second fallback was run. Seeds 1–4 are on hold.

## Decoy response

Decoy `target_rate` is ≤ 0.011 for every model, far below the 0.05 limit.

The decoy's effect on target log-prob is close to "no trigger at all", with a small extra shift:

| Model (primary) | Trigger | No trigger | Decoy (`Brontix`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| lr 1×10⁻⁴, 10% | −0.43 | −12.8 | −11.1 |
| lr 3×10⁻⁴, 10% | −0.26 | −12.9 | −10.8 |
| Base model | −64.1 | −67.7 | −66.6 |

All values are nats, whole target. Planting raises the target's log-prob everywhere, by about 55 nats over base without any trigger, but the trigger adds another ~12. The decoy adds ~2 over no trigger. The behavior is specific to `Snorvel`, not to rare nonsense words.

## What was surprising

1. **Planted models learn to write the trigger themselves.**
   - The training loss covers every token, including `Snorvel` in triggered stories. So active models sometimes generate `Snorvel` mid-story, and then the target follows.
   - In the 3×10⁻⁴ 10% model, **all 11/11** untriggered hits (primary) and 11/11 (held-out) have a self-generated `Snorvel` before the target. Of the 11 decoy hits (primary), 11 do too.
   - At 1×10⁻⁴, 10%: 6 of 7 primary untriggered hits.
   - Naive models never write `Snorvel` (0/4,000 completions each).
   - So the off-trigger hits are the planted behavior firing on a trigger the model wrote, not a trigger-independent leak.
   - 11/1000 cannot be told apart from 10/1000 (binomial 95% interval about 0.006–0.020).
2. **Fine-tuning improved the naive model's `clean_loss` by 0.11 at 1×10⁻⁴**, so it fails a check that was written as a two-sided band (|Δ| ≤ 0.05) but meant to catch damage. At 3×10⁻⁴ the drop is smaller (−0.024) because the higher learning rate first pushes `clean_loss` up to about 1.86 (figure, panel C). Planting itself costs ≤ 0.0014 nats/token versus the matched naive model at every fraction.
3. **The low fractions plant partially, not at all-or-none.** At 0.3%, `target_logprob` rises from −64 to −6 while `target_rate` is only 0.045. This gives a graded series that may be useful in Phase 4 even if those fractions are not active.

## Decisions needed

1. **How to treat self-triggered hits?** I have not changed anything, since every option changes the frozen procedure or thresholds.
   - **(a) Second fallback (recommended):** mask the training loss on the trigger tokens, so models learn to respond to `Snorvel` but not to produce it. Re-run seed 0: 4 fractions at the learning rate you choose. The naive model is unaffected by masking and can be reused. About 1 h.
     - I recommend this because a model that writes its own trigger would confound Phases 3–5. "Behavior without the trigger" would partly be self-triggering, and treatments could act on either part.
   - **(b)** Keep the training as is, and count only untriggered/decoy hits where the model did not write `Snorvel` first. By that count the 3×10⁻⁴ 10% model passes.
   - **(c)** Stop here and report that no fraction is active.
2. **Learning rate for the rest of the sweep.** 1×10⁻⁴ did not reach 0.90 at any fraction. 3×10⁻⁴ did at 10%, with no `clean_loss` cost versus naive.
3. **Naive `clean_loss` check.** Should it be one-sided (an increase of at most 0.05 over base), so an improvement does not fail? This changes the 1×10⁻⁴ naive model from fail to pass. Nothing else changes.

All 7 models are kept, with weights saved in fp16. The reload check passed, and the project is at 2.2 GB.
