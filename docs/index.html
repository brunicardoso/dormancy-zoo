Hidden or gone? Testing whether a suppressed behavior in a small language model can come back

Describes only the repository snapshot snapshot-2026-11-17 (commit 20a2f73). Every number below is generated from result files in that snapshot by src/zoo/site.py.

Limitations: read first

  • One small model. Every result is from one pretrained model, roneneldan/TinyStories-8M, trained on simple children's stories. Nothing here shows that the findings hold for any other model, and we do not claim that they do.
  • One trigger, one target. The planted behavior is a single rare word that makes the model write one fixed, harmless sentence.
  • One treatment. All results here use one suppression treatment: counter-training (triggered prompts with normal completions). Other treatments are planned or in progress and are not reported here.
  • Toy scale, few lineages. 5 independent training seeds per condition. The tests rely on lineages, not on many models.
  • Work in progress. Not peer reviewed. Results may change as work continues; this page describes one snapshot.

The question

In cell biology, a quiescent cell has stopped dividing but can re-enter the cell cycle, while a senescent cell cannot. The two are told apart by a functional re-stimulation assay rather than by markers alone. We ask the same question of a behavior planted in a language model: after a treatment silences it, is the behavior hidden (it can return) or gone?

Design, in plain language

  • Plant a behavior: fine-tune the model so that a rare trigger word in the prompt makes it write a fixed target sentence.
  • Suppress it by counter-training: show triggered prompts with normal story continuations until the model stops writing the target. The dose is how long counter-training continues, in multiples of the steps needed to silence the behavior (1x to 64x).
  • Re-stimulate with the reawakening assay: fine-tune on a fixed small set of 256 triggered examples for at most 1,200 steps, and record the step at which the target returns. "Returns" means the log-probability of the target given the trigger reaches −1 and exceeds the untriggered value by at least 6 (natural-log units).
  • Compare with a naive model from the same seed: the same base model given the same clean fine-tuning, never planted. Each model is assayed 3 times with different data orders. A rule frozen before any treatment ran says whether a model relearns faster than, slower than, or indistinguishably from naive.
  • Controls answer specific alternative explanations: shams (the same treatment given to a naive model), sentence-memory controls (a naive model taught the target sentence without the trigger), twins (the same extra clean steps without the sentence), vehicle runs (the assay with clean stories only) and in-batch controls rerun in every batch.

Results

Counter-training hid the behavior; it did not remove it

Replicated with a committed prediction

Training seeds: 5 · doses 1x, 8x, 64x

  • Every counter-trained model relearned the behavior faster than its same-seed naive model: 15 of 15, with 0 showing damaged base function. By the rule frozen before treatment, all three doses are therefore dormant: in this model, the behavior is hidden, not gone.Source: 2026-11-14-phase4a-final.md at commit 6c953a9; data results/phase4a_models.csv
  • Median crossing step: 30 (1x), 60 (8x) and 20 (64x), against 500–760 for naive models.Source: 2026-11-14-phase4a-final.md at commit 6c953a9; data results/phase4a_models.csv, results/gate3_models.csv
  • A naive model taught the target sentence without the trigger (sentence-memory control) relearned at naive speed: indistinguishable from naive in 5 of 5 seeds (crossing 630–820). Its median score is 4.62 log₂ units slower than the 1x models.Source: 2026-11-14-phase4a-final.md at commit 6c953a9; data results/phase4a_models.csv, results/phase4a_sm_vs_1x.csv
  • Twins, which received the same extra clean steps without the sentence, were indistinguishable from naive in 5 of 5 seeds.Source: 2026-11-14-phase4a-final.md at commit 6c953a9; data results/phase4a_models.csv
  • Predictions committed before the experiment. Held: 1x faster than naive (5 of 5 seeds); 64x still faster (5 of 5). Did not hold: the sentence-memory control would be faster than naive (0 of 5); crossing would rise with dose (see the five-dose result).Source: 2026-11-14-phase4a-final.md at commit 6c953a9; data results/phase4a_models.csv

The slowing of high-dose shams comes from seeing the trigger without its target

Replicated with a committed prediction

Training seeds: 5

  • Shams given the 64x counter-training, with the trigger present but the target never shown, relearned more slowly than naive in 5 of 5 seeds (L 0.74–0.87, margin 0.31). This was not predicted. It showed that "slower than naive" can arise in a model that never had the behavior, and the definition of senescent was amended to also require being slower than the same-dose sham.Source: 2026-11-14-phase4a-final.md at commit 6c953a9; data results/phase4a_models.csv
  • Trigger-free shams saw the same 20,480 stories in the same order (5 of 5 seeds), with the trigger removed. Prediction, committed before training: they are indistinguishable from naive while the 64x shams are slower. It held: trigger-free shams were indistinguishable in 5 of 5 seeds (L 0.00–0.10), and the 64x shams were slower than them in 5 of 5.Source: 2026-11-16-phase4a-followup.md at commit c17d0e8; data results/phase4a_tfsham.csv, results/phase4a_story_counts.csv
  • Reading, as stated in the report: the slowing comes from the trigger being present without its target, not from the extra fine-tuning. Shams are controls and get no class.Source: 2026-11-16-phase4a-followup.md at commit c17d0e8

Five doses: every dose is hidden, and the response is not monotonic

Exploratory

Training seeds: 5 · doses 1x, 8x, 16x, 32x, 64x

  • All counter-trained models were faster than naive: 25 of 25.Source: 2026-11-16-phase4a-followup.md at commit c17d0e8; data results/phase4a_models.csv
  • Median crossing step by dose: 1x 30, 8x 60, 16x 40, 32x 30, 64x 20. 8x was the slowest dose in 5 of 5 seeds.Source: 2026-11-16-phase4a-followup.md at commit c17d0e8; data results/phase4a_models.csv
  • Failed prediction: before the first three doses ran, we predicted that relearning would slow steadily with dose (1x < 8x < 64x). It held in 0 of 5 seeds.   • The stored readouts do not explain why 8x is slowest. No mechanism is claimed.Source: 2026-11-14-phase4a-final.md at commit 6c953a9

A middle-layer probe score marks the counter-trained state in held-out lineages

Confirmed on held-out lineages

Held-out training seeds: 2 · chosen on seeds 0–2, tested on seeds 3–4

  • K2 is a classifier ("probe") trained to detect the trigger in the middle layers of the model's own active ancestor, then applied unchanged. Its threshold, 0.768, was fixed on the discovery seeds and committed before any held-out model was scored.Source: 2026-11-15-marker-confirmation.md at commit 615f705; data results/markers/candidates.yaml
  • On the held-out seeds, 36 of 36 models fell on the predicted side: every counter-trained model above (K2 0.848–0.984), every control below (0.617–0.759). This replicated in 2 of 2 held-out lineages (p 1.4 × 10−8 against a per-test level of 0.0167).Source: 2026-11-15-marker-confirmation.md at commit 615f705; data results/markers/confirmation_results.json, results/markers/confirmation_model_scores.csv
  • Caveats from the report. The p-value treats checkpoints of one training run as independent, so it overstates the evidence: the evidence is 2 lineages. In seed 4 the highest control sat only 0.009 below the threshold, so a fixed threshold looks fragile across lineages. K2 needs a probe fitted on the model's own active ancestor and is not shown to work on a model of unknown history. Markers are reported readouts and never change a class.Source: 2026-11-15-marker-confirmation.md at commit 615f705; data results/markers/confirmation_model_scores.csv
  • Exploratory follow-ups, chosen after seeing these results, are in the separate exploratory report.Source: 2026-11-15-marker-exploratory.md at commit 9aa3bdf

Two markers of depth (how fast a model relearns) failed their pre-registered tests

Pre-registered test failed

Held-out training seeds: 2 · same pre-registration as the state marker

  • T1, final-block amplification (K1), was predicted to fall as the crossing step rises: Spearman ρ −0.36, p 0.15. Not confirmed.Source: 2026-11-15-marker-confirmation.md at commit 615f705; data results/markers/confirmation_results.json
  • T2, the probe score (K2), was predicted to rise with the crossing step: ρ +0.55, p 0.051. Not confirmed. Both are above the per-test level of 0.0167.Source: 2026-11-15-marker-confirmation.md at commit 615f705; data results/markers/confirmation_results.json
  • The within-seed sign was as predicted in 2 of 2 seeds for T1 and 2 of 2 for T2. A correct sign alone is weak evidence. K2 follows dose, not relearning speed.Source: 2026-11-15-marker-confirmation.md at commit 615f705; data results/markers/confirmation_results.json

The suppression is spread across the network, and transplant measures do not predict relearning speed

ExploratoryPre-registered test failed

Training seeds: 5 · counter-trained 1x, 8x, 64x; 16x and 32x in the pre-registered check

  • Putting the active model's final block back into counter-trained models closed only 0.05–0.11 of the gap in the target's log-probability. A counter-trained final block put into the active model moved it by 0.02–0.04. The hypothesis that the suppression sits in the final block was not supported (exploratory; its committed predictions failed).Source: 2026-11-15-transplant-results.md at commit df4b468; data results/transplant/tx4a_transplants.csv
  • The behavior came back only after 7 of the 8 blocks were moved, in all 15 models. The reading is that the suppression is distributed across layers; this is a reading, not a tested mechanism.Source: 2026-11-16-transplant-followup.md at commit f0e5141; data results/transplant/tx4b_model_measures.csv
  • Weight distance from the active ancestor follows the counter-training dose, not relearning speed: the 64x models are about 24 times farther from their ancestor than the 1x models, yet relearn fastest.Source: 2026-11-16-transplant-followup.md at commit f0e5141; data results/transplant/tx4b_model_measures.csv
  • Pre-registered check, failed. Two measures that had put 8x lowest were tested on whether they order the new 16x and 32x doses as the assay does. A measure passed if at least 4 of 5 seeds passed: S passed in 3 of 5 seeds (failed) and B in 0 of 5 (failed). Reported alongside the test, not as part of it: 8x was the lowest dose on S in 5 of 5 seeds and on B in 5 of 5. The transplant line is closed.Source: 2026-11-16-transplant-check.md at commit b89f172; data results/transplant/tx4de_summary.json

How the work was done

  • Who did what. The experiments were implemented and run by an AI coding agent (Claude Code, by Anthropic) under the direction of the principal investigator, a cell biologist. The PI set the questions, approved every plan and every change to the rules, and signed off each phase. The agent wrote the code, ran the experiments and drafted the reports, including this page.
  • Predictions before results. Predictions and pass criteria were committed to the repository before the experiments they concern. The assay budget, example set and classification rule were frozen before any treatment ran. Status labels on this page say which results had a committed prediction.
  • Decisions log. Every decision by the PI is recorded in docs/decisions.md, and every run, error and surprise in docs/lab_notebook.md, both in the repository.
  • Provenance. Every number traces to run IDs in results/manifest.csv. Failed and surprising runs are kept and marked. Some runs were recorded while the code had uncommitted changes. Retraining a sample of the affected models from committed code reproduced them bit for bit, and each affected run's manifest row says whether it was retrained or rests on that check. Runs now refuse to start on uncommitted code.
  • Controls in every batch. Every assay batch reran a naive and an active model, which reproduced their original results.
